# Cross-bank findings

## 1. Difficulty defect
Current reserve design is explicitly **36 easy / 36 medium / 18 hard**. That architecture no longer fits the current ASCYN PRO difficult-assessment standard.

Implementation rule:
- Do not relabel easy questions as hard.
- The 54 rewrite IDs must earn difficulty through applied scenarios, plausible same-domain distractors, sequencing/priority, label/regulation logic, or safety decision making.
- The 36 retained bodies are already sufficiently useful to preserve; their explanations can be upgraded without changing their keys.

## 2. Giveaway distractors
A recurring defect is one correct professional answer against joke, irrelevant, or obviously unsafe alternatives. Examples include price lists, music, ribbons, taste-testing disinfectant, social-media posting, and similar noncompetitive options.

Implementation must use distractors that a partially prepared student could realistically choose.

## 3. Repetition
The reserve overweights:
- clean-before-disinfect
- label/contact time
- processed-vs-used storage
- hand hygiene
- generic blood-exposure cleanup
- OSHA/EPA name recall
- generic “decline service” logic

The rewrite plan uses those slots to strengthen under-tested Milady objectives instead of repeating the same knowledge at lower difficulty.

## 4. Milady coverage gaps to strengthen
The rewrites should deliberately add or strengthen:
- active vs inactive bacterial stages
- local vs systemic infection
- immunity/resistance
- disinfectant classes/label claims without oversimplified hierarchies
- autoclave/spore-testing context
- water/electrical/GFCI safety
- damaged equipment
- heat/fire and trip hazards
- ventilation
- current state-rule/licensing responsibility
- hazard/emergency information
- porous/multiuse/barrier handling

## 5. NIC alignment
NIC's Barber Theory outline places infection control/safety under Scientific Concepts and specifically calls for disease transmission, sanitation/cleansing, disinfection, sterilization, contamination/cross-contamination, blood exposure procedures, and government-agency requirements.

The reserve should therefore remain broad across those areas rather than collapsing into sanitation-only recall.

## 6. 2026 blood-exposure guardrail
Because NIC states that blood-exposure procedures are being updated beginning March 2026, the reserve must not certify a stale memorized sequence as universally current.

During implementation:
- verify any exact procedural step against the current controlling NIC material available at that time
- otherwise test durable safety principles and explicitly defer to current procedure/applicable rules
