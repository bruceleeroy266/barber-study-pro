# C8-3 — Lesson Content Hardening

## Scope

C8-3 hardens **only the Chapter 8 lesson**.

The existing 50 flashcards and 30-question assessment are deliberately unchanged in this phase.

## Source discipline

Primary content basis:
- Chapter 8 source pages 206–217.

Exam-domain alignment:
- current NIC Barber Theory materials list basic electricity, electrical measurements, electrotherapy, light therapy, facial/skin procedures, and safety as relevant barber theory competencies.

The textbook source controls the chapter's terminology and subject coverage. NIC is used only to confirm competency relevance, not to invent source details or reproduce exam questions.

## What was preserved

The hardened lesson continues to teach:

- electricity as energy
- electric current, conductors, insulators, and complete circuits
- direct current, alternating current, converters, and rectifiers
- volts, amperes, milliamperes, ohms, watts, kilowatts, and kilowatt-hours
- fuses, circuit breakers, grounding, ground-fault protection, and equipment inspection
- electrotherapy terminology
- polarity, anode, cathode, and electrodes
- galvanic-current source concepts
- microcurrent and Tesla high-frequency source concepts
- electromagnetic spectrum
- wavelength, frequency, visible light, ultraviolet, and infrared
- light therapy, LEDs, therapeutic lamps, and laser terminology
- client protection, eye protection, supervision, and scope-of-practice awareness

## Hardening corrections

### 1. Removed fake credential language

The lesson no longer presents completion as:
- an electrical-safety certification,
- an electrical credential,
- authority to repair wiring,
- authority to interpret electrical code.

Chapter 8 is an academic barbering lesson, not an electrician credential.

### 2. Removed state-board certainty claims

"BOARD EXAM ALERT" was replaced with neutral "EXAM FOCUS" language.

The lesson no longer claims:
- a concept appears on every state board exam,
- missing a listed concept means failure,
- the lesson can predict exact exam questions.

### 3. Corrected converter / rectifier language

The source distinguishes:
- converter: DC → AC
- rectifier: AC → DC

The lesson now preserves that distinction instead of saying either device generically converts both directions.

### 4. Hardened electrical-hazard language

Removed:
- "water + electricity = death"
- one-number fatal-current rules
- simplistic voltage-lethality comparisons
- claims that a warm cord proves one specific electrical fault
- statements implying insulating materials guarantee protection

Replaced with multi-factor shock-risk reasoning and stop-use/equipment-evaluation guidance.

### 5. Hardened electrical load guidance

The lesson still teaches watts, volts, amperes, and circuit-load reasoning, but it no longer encourages barbers to redesign or redistribute building circuits.

Repeated breaker trips, damaged connections, unusual heating, or facility electrical problems are escalation points for qualified personnel.

### 6. Hardened GFCI / grounding / listing language

The textbook source describes GFI/GFCI protection near sinks and water.

The runtime lesson now avoids turning a source statement into a universal jurisdictional legal claim. It teaches the protective purpose and directs learners to applicable electrical code/facility requirements.

Grounding remains a safety concept without "save your life" guarantees.

Equipment certification/listing is described as recognized third-party evaluation rather than "no mark = no trust."

### 7. Hardened electrotherapy scope

The source explicitly says electrotherapy modalities depend on state licensing rules.

The runtime lesson now makes this boundary prominent:
- terminology knowledge does not authorize performance,
- state scope matters,
- device instructions matter,
- client condition and screening matter,
- training matters.

### 8. Hardened galvanic claims

The textbook's polarity effects remain represented as **source-described modality concepts**.

The lesson no longer turns them into universal:
- treatment sequences,
- guaranteed pore/tissue outcomes,
- universal product-polarity rules,
- universal contraindication lists,
- generic sensitivity-test requirements.

Unexpected pain, burning, stinging, or excessive discomfort is now a stop-service signal.

### 9. Hardened microcurrent and high-frequency claims

The source lists multiple cosmetic/physiologic effects.

The runtime lesson now labels those as source-described modality claims rather than guaranteed results for every client/device.

### 10. Hardened light and UV language

The electromagnetic-spectrum concepts remain intact.

The lesson no longer:
- calls UV cabinets/lights a substitute for required infection-control processes,
- presents one fixed device distance as universal,
- presents one fixed exposure duration as universal,
- diagnoses or treats infection as a barber service,
- turns source-era therapeutic claims into guaranteed outcomes.

### 11. Hardened LED claims

The source associations for blue/red/yellow/green LED remain recognizable.

The lesson now requires:
- device-specific indication,
- client suitability,
- permitted scope,
- no guaranteed treatment outcome.

### 12. Hardened laser language

Selective photothermolysis remains as the source concept.

Removed:
- outdated/unnecessary universal device-class statement,
- broad claim that most states prohibit barber laser operation.

Replaced with:
- do not assume barber licensure authorizes laser operation,
- verify current state law/credentialing/facility/device requirements.

### 13. Hardened light-therapy safety

The source includes eye protection, supervision, client comfort, product contraindication review, and example treatment distance/time.

The runtime lesson preserves the safety principles while replacing universal fixed numbers with:
- manufacturer/device-specific operating distance,
- manufacturer/device-specific exposure time,
- required eye protection,
- continuous supervision,
- stop-service response to burning/pain/excessive heat/adverse reactions,
- medication/photosensitivity/contraindication screening.

## Explicit non-goals

C8-3 does **not**:
- rewrite the 50 flashcards,
- rewrite the 30-question assessment,
- create micro checks,
- create reassessment reserve questions,
- wire instructor diagnostics,
- change Chapter 8 grading weights.

Those remain later phases.

## C8-3 exit criteria

C8-3 closes only when:
- lesson guardrail tests pass;
- mapped source concepts remain present;
- no banned overclaim patterns remain in the lesson;
- flashcard and assessment counts remain unchanged;
- Engineering Verification is GREEN;
- Vercel is READY on the exact C8-3 head.

After C8-3, begin **C8-4 — Flashcard Hardening & Coverage Expansion**.
