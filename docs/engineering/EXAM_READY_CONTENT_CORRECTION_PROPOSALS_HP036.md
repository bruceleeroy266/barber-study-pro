## Corrections to consider — no source changes

### HS-051 — Ringed hair
- **Original issue:** Question attributes alternating 'gray and pigmented' bands to a variation of canities. The cited textbook chapter describes this association, but independent dermatology literature describes light/dark banding associated with optical differences (pili annulati), not necessarily alternating melanin.
- **Proposed replacement prompt:** “A hair shaft displays alternating light and dark bands along its length. Which description best identifies this appearance?”
- **Proposed best choice:** “Ringed hair (pili annulati).”
- **Proposed explanation:** “Ringed hair has a banded appearance along the shaft. That visual pattern does not, by itself, establish alternating pigment deposits.”
- **Risk:** Moderate factual/terminological; moderate originality risk because the original stem/explanation closely recites textbook language. Proposed independent expression still needs documented review.

### HP-012 — Integrated hair analysis
- **Original issue:** Existing correct option implies coarse texture can justify stronger chemical processing. The assembled bank also has a hardening override that **retains** this oversimplification.
- **Proposed replacement prompt:** “A client's hair is coarse, highly porous, and has low elasticity. Which assessment is most appropriate before a chemical service?”
- **Proposed best choice:** “Consider all findings; rapid absorption and poor elasticity call for caution, following product instructions and appropriate compatibility/strand testing before deciding whether to proceed.”
- **Proposed explanation:** “Strand diameter alone does not prescribe strength or processing time. Porosity and elasticity help assess how damaged or fragile hair may respond; follow manufacturer instructions and refrain when hair condition makes the service unsafe.”
- **Risk:** Elevated safety/pedagogical risk; moderate expression risk from textbook-dependent phrasing. Redraft all distractors to make one best answer; do not insert without review.

### HP-020 — Salt bonds
- **Original issue:** Source explanation asserts a numerical share of hair strength without a separately documented supporting basis; concept overlaps HP-038.
- **Proposed replacement explanation:** “Salt bonds are ionic side bonds in hair. Changes in pH can disrupt these interactions; they may form again as pH returns to a suitable range.”
- **Proposed key:** A — Salt bond (unchanged).
- **Risk:** Low-to-moderate factual risk for unverified quantification; moderate bank-quality risk for near-repeat with HP-038. Evaluate deduplication **before** modifying any source.
