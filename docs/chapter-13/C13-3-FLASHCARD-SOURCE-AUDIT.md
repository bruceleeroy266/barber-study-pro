# C13-3 — 90-Flashcard Source + Concept Hardening

## Scope and source

All 90 active Chapter 13 flashcards were independently reviewed against the connected **Milady Standard Barbering, Chapter 13 — Shaving and Facial-Hair Design, printed pages 338–354**.

C13-3 keeps the existing 90-card bank and stable IDs. It does not add cards merely to force symmetry.

Milady supplies the factual/terminology reference. ASCYN PRO supplies original student-facing wording.

## Result

- Active cards: **90**
- Stable IDs preserved: **90/90**
- Order indexes preserved: **1–90**
- Canonical concept mappings preserved: **90/90**
- Duplicate fronts: **0**
- Duplicate backs: **0**
- Pre-C13-3 difficulty: **69 Easy / 21 Medium / 0 Hard**
- Post-C13-3 difficulty: **52 Easy / 30 Medium / 8 Hard**

The eight Hard cards are existing IDs converted to application/safety reasoning; no card-count inflation occurred.

## Repair principles

Cards were changed only where the review found one or more of:

- source-proximate textbook wording
- medical/diagnostic overreach
- unsafe universalization of a procedure
- jurisdictional rule stated as universal
- close-shave risk overstated
- rigid face-shape prescription presented as a rule
- weak recall-only wording where application/safety reasoning was more appropriate
- ambiguity or awkward grammar

Short technical terms and concise factual labels were retained when they are the terminology itself.

## High-risk repairs

### Medical scope

Cards 003, 012, and 014 now distinguish observation from diagnosis/treatment. Pseudofolliculitis, folliculitis, and keloid-prone reactions remain teachable concepts, but the barber is not told to diagnose them.

### Ingrown-hair / grain decisions

Cards 011–018 now emphasize actual growth pattern, conservative technique, and grain re-checking rather than treating a diagram or one direction as universally correct.

### Razor handling and stroke control

Cards 021, 024, 026, 028, 030–032, and 039–049 were checked against pp. 342–346. Source-supported facts such as point-leading glide, approximately 30-degree-or-less angle, short controlled strokes, dry support fingers, opposite-direction stretch, and stroke-length/speed control were preserved in ASCYN PRO wording.

### Shave types

Cards 051–054 retain first-time-over, second-time-over, once-over, and close-shave distinctions. Card 054 no longer claims that against-grain shaving automatically causes infection; it focuses on irritation/ingrown-hair risk and client/scope/rule judgment.

### Mustache and beard design

Cards 060–067 preserve proportion, growth, density, texture, natural hairlines, gradual trimming, and client preference. Rigid "face type X = mandatory mustache Y" recall cards were converted into design-principle/application cards.

### Infection control / exposure response

Cards 071–077 preserve sharps disposal, standard precautions, chair stability, safe lather/moisture management, skin support, and client-comfort factors. Card 072 is now an urgent blood-exposure decision card.

### Regulation and styptic

Cards 083–085 no longer present one state's razor/glove rules as universal. They direct learners to current jurisdictional requirements and keep styptic use tied to sanitary handling and exposure procedures.
