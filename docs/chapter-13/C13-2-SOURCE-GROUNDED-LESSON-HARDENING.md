## Source-grounded hardening performed

### Introduction and purpose — p. 338

The legacy lesson used a near-source quotation about the professional shave and repeated textbook-style "why study" phrasing.

Repairs:

- replaced the near-verbatim quote with original ASCYN PRO language
- preserved the professional-shave / client-comfort concept
- preserved facial-feature and professional-skill relevance without copying textbook prose

### Fundamental guidelines / hair type — pp. 338–339

Repairs:

- rewrote the shave objective and preparation discussion
- preserved skin analysis, grain analysis, towel cautions, post-shave facial caution, sensitive-area care, product selection, heavy-beard preparation, and pre-shave mustache shaping
- converted medical-adjacent pseudofolliculitis / folliculitis / keloid wording into barber-scope language
- added explicit "do not diagnose" boundaries
- avoided implying that the barber determines medical status

### Hair growth / customer satisfaction / 14 areas — p. 340

Repairs:

- preserved grain, grain change, whorl/growth-pattern concepts
- clarified that the 14-area map is a training guide and client-specific grain still governs stroke choice
- rewrote customer-satisfaction material into original quality-control language
- retained state-rule variability rather than presenting glove rules as universal

### 14-area map and shaving movement table — pp. 341–342

Repairs:

- retained all source-supported area/stroke relationships
- paraphrased procedural descriptions so ASCYN PRO does not reproduce the table's wording
- preserved right-/left-handed mirroring concept

### Razor positions, stroke control, skin stretching — pp. 342–346

Repairs:

- preserved freehand, backhand, reverse-freehand, and reverse-backhand distinctions
- clarified that facial shaving primarily uses the first three while reverse-backhand is associated with neck/outline work
- rewrote grip/stroke summaries and skin-stretching guidance
- preserved source-supported light pressure, point-leading movement, dry support hand, opposite-direction stretch, stroke length/speed concepts, and growth-pattern adaptation without copying source sentences

### Body positioning — p. 347

Repairs:

- retained right-/left-handed starting-side logic
- retained the four right-handed training positions and associated shaving areas
- rewrote position labels/descriptions in ASCYN PRO voice
- emphasized moving the body instead of overreaching

### Professional shave and shave types — pp. 348–349

Repairs:

- preserved preparation → shaving → finishing architecture
- removed source-proximate sequencing prose
- softened physiologic claims around steaming; retained beard-softening/preparation purpose
- preserved first-time-over, second-time-over, once-over, and close-shave terminology
- changed close-shave wording from a broad infection claim to cautious irritation / ingrown-hair risk language
- made close-shave appropriateness conditional on client, skin, growth pattern, training, and local rules

### Neck / outline shave — p. 349

Repairs:

- preserved service boundaries and skin inspection
- rephrased moles/warts/raised-area guidance
- avoided implying diagnosis

### Mustache / beard design — pp. 349–351

Repairs:

- preserved facial-proportion, growth-density, natural-hairline, gradual-trimming, tool-selection, and client-consultation concepts
- removed rigid source-derived face-type prescriptions from the student lesson
- replaced them with proportion principles and explicit client-preference guidance
- removed value-laden "flaws / less desirable features" wording

### Infection control and safety — p. 352

Repairs:

- converted the source table into a shorter original ASCYN PRO checklist
- preserved hand hygiene, reusable-tool preparation, sharps disposal, clean linens/barriers, cut/nick exposure procedure, chair locking, grain-aware first pass, safe skin stretching, moisture/lather management, and controlled stroke follow-through
- distinguished reusable razor components from disposable replaceable blades more clearly

### Razor anatomy / handling — pp. 353–354

Repairs:

- retained the source-supported razor-part vocabulary and handling-learning purpose
- rewrote explanatory prose in ASCYN PRO voice
- kept detailed hands-on straight-razor procedure outside this lesson-hardening pass rather than copying step-by-step textbook directions

### Regulatory wording

The legacy lesson closely repeated the textbook's state-regulatory alert.

Replacement rule:

> Razor type, glove use, and related requirements vary by jurisdiction. ASCYN PRO presents technique; current local board/school/shop rules govern legal practice.

No universal state-law claim is made.

### Styptic wording — p. 352

Repairs:

- preserved alum/astringent/minor-bleeding purpose
- removed unnecessary medical-style terminology
- tied any use to sanitation, regulation, and exposure procedures

## Anti-copy rule

Milady is the source of truth for the educational concept.

ASCYN PRO must express that concept independently.

C13-2 certification includes regression checks that reject several source-proximate phrases found in the pre-hardening lesson. Short technical terms, chapter titles, named razor positions, and necessary procedural labels are retained where they are the terminology itself.
